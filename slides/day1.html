Who owes what

Producers promise; the consumer states what it needs

The producer owes

The agreed columns, codes and units; deliveries on time; notice before a change; an answer when a delivery fails a rule.

The consumer owes

A written statement of what the analysis needs; the rules it will apply; no silent "fixes" of a producer's data downstream.

What goes wrong. Each consumer quietly repairs each producer in its own code. Three studies, three different repairs, and nobody tells the producer.

Single-table contracts are assumed from DS-311 (or equivalent). What is new here is several producers for one entity: the contract becomes the place where their differences are resolved once, in the open, instead of in every consumer's notebook.

A contract

A contract promises meaning, not only columns

  • Schema: names, types, required fields, keys.
  • Meaning: code sets, units, plausible ranges.
  • Quality: rules every delivery must pass.
  • Service: freshness and frequency.
  • People: the owner, the consumers, who approves a change.

The test

If Rawdah sent pounds tomorrow, would the contract say it was wrong? A list of typed columns would not.

Recap from the single-table course in one breath, then the new question: with three producers, where do their differences live? Answer: in the contract, per producer, in clauses everyone can read.

An open standard

ODCS v3.2.0: the contract is a YAML document anyone can read

apiVersion: v3.2.0
kind: DataContract
id: rimah-encounter
version: 0.1.0
schema:
  - name: encounter
    properties:
      - name: weight_kg
        logicalType: number
        description: Body weight in kilograms.
slaProperties:
  - property: latency
    value: 2
    unit: d

The Open Data Contract Standard is published by the Bitol project; v3.2.0 is the version on its site this month. Show the sections: fundamentals, schema, quality, SLA, team, roles, custom properties. Pandera and Soda Core are other ways to express and run rules; the contract can carry a Soda block for another engine (the stretch). Sources: the ODCS site and its full example.

Custom properties. ODCS has no section for per-producer mappings; customProperties is where the standard lets you add one. Say so in the contract.

The course helper U.conform reads only these clauses. That is the point: the mapping is data in the contract, not code in a pipeline. A new consumer reads the same clauses. The disposition codes were confirmed by the producers in the case; in real life this is the email you send after Lab 1.1.

The constraint

Health data is sensitive data under the Personal Data Protection Law. The study needs the link, not the identity.

1,953 is the number of true cross-producer pairs in data/truth/person_map.csv, which only the course has. Sources: Personal Data Protection Law (health data is listed among sensitive data in its definitions).

Words that matter

A pseudonym is still personal data; anonymous data is not

PseudonymisedAnonymised
Who can re-identifywhoever holds the extra informationnobody, irreversibly
Still personal datayesno
At Rimahthe linkage keya release that passes Day 3 and 4 tests
The extra information is kept apart. SDAIA's guideline asks that what is needed to re-identify is held separately, under controls.

Paraphrased from SDAIA's Personal Data Destruction, Anonymization and Pseudonymisation Guideline: pseudonymised data remains personal data because it can be linked back with additional information, which must be stored separately and protected; anonymised data must be irreversible and needs an assessment of re-identification. That is why this course never calls a hashed identifier "anonymous". Sources: the guideline, sdaia.gov.sa.

Lab 1.3 · step 1

A plain hash of an identifier is reversed by enumeration

2.2 s
to reverse one SHA-256 of a national identifier when the first four digits are known
  • About 175,000 hashes a second on one laptop core, in plain Python.
  • All 2 billion candidates: about 3 hours on one core; far less on a graphics card.
  • The identifier's space is small and structured: hashing hides nothing.

Numbers from the course's full-mode run of Lab 1.3 on a laptop; participants will see their own. The DS-309 stretch asked whether a hashed identifier is still personal data; today the room sees the answer in seconds.

Lab 1.3 · step 3

A keyed hash with a key the consortium never holds

Linkage unitcreates a key for this project
→
Each producernormalises, then HMAC-SHA-256
→
Consortiumreceives pseudonyms only
  • Without the key nobody can recompute or enumerate the pseudonyms.
  • A new project gets a new key: its pseudonyms cannot be joined with this one's.

The lab uses a demonstration key written in the notebook; say clearly that a real key lives with a linkage unit and travels to producers over a secure channel. Bloom-filter encodings (Schnell and colleagues, 2009) allow approximate matching of names under a key; they have known attacks and are out of scope here.

Day 1 · wrap-up

Three things to keep

Meaning fails silently

Units and codes pass type checks. Profile the distributions and write the fix into the contract.

Link, do not identify

Normalise, then a keyed hash with a key held elsewhere. Measure recall and precision.

Change has a process

Breaking or additive by policy; versions, notice, approval, a parallel run.

Ask each table for the clause they would add first if they ran a feed with several producers at work. Collect two answers.
