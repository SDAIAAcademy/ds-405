Every package: contracts and pipeline, the protected or synthetic data, the utility and attack (or error) report, and the decision record.

Each participant chooses one, individually. R-A and R-B lean on Day 3, R-C on Day 4 session 2; all three need Days 1 and 2 for the data they start from and Day 4 session 4 for the decision. The labs' artifacts are the starting points: decision.yaml, ledger.json, attacks.csv, utility.csv.

How presentations are scored

The rubric

Contracts and pipeline
25%
Synthetic data validation
25%
Privacy method and risk
25%
Release decision
15%
Reproducibility
10%
Show results from at least two measured runs. A claim without a number is a plan, not a result.

Full marks, criterion by criterion. Contracts and pipeline: an ODCS contract that runs on the input, a load proven idempotent by fingerprint, the version recorded. Synthetic data validation (or, for R-C, the error of the released statistics): TSTR against TRTR and the study's estimate with its interval. Privacy method and risk: per-table attacks against a holdout, or a ledger with a stated unit and bound; longitudinal risk measured. Release decision: the checklist against the sharing policy and secondary-use rules, a residual risk with numbers, checkable conditions, a named approver. Reproducibility: a fresh clone runs end to end with the README's commands.

Format

10 minutes for each defence, questions included

1 · The request

Which one, what it needs exactly, and the rules it was tested against.

2 · The package

Method, utility and risk numbers, the decision and its conditions.

3 · The live attack

The assessor names a table; you re-run one attack on it from your repository.

Peer feedback. After each defence, everyone writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.

The live re-run is the reproducibility test in miniature: the attack must run from the participant's repository in under a minute. For R-C, the "attack" is the averaging attack or a differencing of two released tables against their ledger. Stop questions at the ten-minute mark.
